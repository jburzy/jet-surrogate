How it works

  1. 1

    Pick a preserved analysis

    Each entry in our library is a published search with its own surrogate, a model card and validation plots. Browse the library.

  2. 2

    Upload your HepMC file

    Generator-level events of your signal model in HepMC2 or HepMC3 format, gzipped if you like. No detector simulation needed.

  3. 3

    Get the efficiency

    The signal-region efficiency with its statistical uncertainty, plus per-event probabilities in an HDF5 file you can use in your own limit setting.

Why surrogates

Reinterpretation asks a simple question: would a published search also have seen my model? Answering it needs the analysis selection, and modern searches increasingly make that selection with machine-learned classifiers that read low-level detector inputs such as tracks and calorimeter clusters. The response of such a classifier to a new model cannot be evaluated without a full detector simulation, which is exactly what people outside the collaboration do not have.

A surrogate is a second model, trained by the analysis team on the same simulated samples, that learns to reproduce the analysis decision from generator truth alone. Given the stable particles and decay vertices of an event it predicts the probability that the event would enter the signal region. Averaged over your events, that probability is the efficiency you need. Each surrogate ships with a validation section that shows how well it tracks the full analysis across the models it was tested on, so you can judge how far to trust it for yours.

We host the library and run the surrogates. Analysis teams add their preserved analyses by pull request. Learn how to contribute one.
